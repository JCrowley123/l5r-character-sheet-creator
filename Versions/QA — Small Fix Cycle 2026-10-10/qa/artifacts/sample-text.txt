HISTORY / BACKGROUND
Kakita Sairyū, now twenty years old, is widely regarded as a prodigy of the blade. Freshly appointed as an instructor 
at the Kakita Dueling Academy—an honour granted only a short time after his Genpuku—he stands among the 
youngest teachers ever to hold such a position. His early duels and successful missions have already earned him a 
reputation as a rising star of the Kakita, and his family’s expectations for him are nothing short of extraordinary.
From childhood, Sairyū trained alongside his closest friend, Kakita Kaguhana. She was a strikingly talented duelist 
in her own right—swift, sharp‑minded, and capable of matching Sairyū blow for blow. On more than one occasion 
she bested him, and their long history together gradually shaped a friendly rivalry that pushed them both to excel.
As they grew into their teenage years, that rivalry softened into affection, and affection into a quiet, secret romance. 
They chose to keep their relationship hidden, unwilling to let gossip interfere with their shared ambition to become 
the finest duelists of their generation.
That future ended abruptly.
During a mission in Lion territory, Kaguhana was challenged to a duel by a Lion samurai. Sairyū saw immediately 
that she was outmatched and urged her to refuse, but Kaguhana—driven by pride, honour, and the fierce spirit that 
had always defined her—accepted. The duel was swift, and Kaguhana fell. Her life ended before Sairyū had a chance 
to process what had happened.
In the aftermath, Sairyū’s grief nearly consumed him. Every part of him longed to avenge her, to challenge the Lion 
samurai and spill his blood on the earth where Kaguhana died. But even in his anguish, Sairyū recognised the truth: 
he was in no state to fight. Rage would cloud his judgement, and he would die a meaningless death—one that would 
dishonour Kaguhana’s memory rather than honour it.
Instead, he carried her to the nearest temple so that the priests could look after her body as he completed his mission 
with solemn resolve.  Once he had achieved his goal Sairyū made the long lonely journey home, there he laid her to 
rest.  
He never forgot the face of the man who killed her.
Since that day, Sairyū has devoted himself to a new philosophy. He refuses to succumb to the pride and 
overconfidence that claimed Kaguhana’s life, and he teaches his students the same lesson. He is weary of seeing 
promising young samurai throw their lives away in duels born of ego rather than purpose. To Sairyū, a duel is sacred, 
and a samurai’s life is precious. A samurai is a weapon of the Emperor—meant to serve, not to be squandered.
Through discipline, humility, and mastery of the blade, Sairyū strives to honour Kaguhana’s memory and ensure that 
no student of his repeats the fatal mistake she made.

How a player uses it

  1. Questions. From "Help me choose" on the wizard's Clan step, or "Not sure what to play?" on the Characters screen. One question per screen, each with "No preference", and "See my results now" after question 9.
  2. Results. One or two best matches and four more worth a look. If the answers say too little, a sample of six Schools to start from instead.
  3. Guide. Who they are, how they play, how to roleplay them with a few optional questions to think about, a suggested starting build, and where they can go later.
  4. Build. The wizard applies Clan, Family and School and carries on at Rings & Traits, with folded "Suggested for…" hints on the next steps.

Reliability

Reliable here means: a player who roughly knows what they want finds it in the top six about 9 times in 10, and no School is hidden from the players it suits. That is three questions, and these tests answer only the first.

  1. Does the scoring do what the ratings say? Measured below.
  2. Are the ratings right? Simulation can't tell, because the simulated players are built from the same ratings. See Checking the ratings.
  3. Do real players like what they get? Needs real people: your group, then a couple of new players before any release.

The tests run in about a minute. Rules must always hold. Floors sit at today's values, so a change that makes things worse fails instead of slipping through. "A player who wants School X" means 20 simulated players per School who pick the answer that suits it best 60% of the time.

Why tuning can't reach 90%

Trying different settings only moved "finds it in the top six" between 77% and 81%. What's left is in the ratings: near-twin Schools with almost the same labels (Goju Ninja and Shosuro Infiltrator, Agasha and Tamori, many monk orders), and labels that look wrong. For example, a player who answers only "a sword, the front line, the Wall" gets the Boar and Badger Schools ahead of Hida Bushi, because the draft gave them a "Wall" home. The book step fixed that one: Hida Bushi now comes first. Better ratings are the way to 90%, and the rest of the gap is mostly near-twin monk orders that only your group's judgement calls can separate.

Checking the ratings without an expert

Approved on 25 November. It doesn't rely on anyone's expertise. It turns each rating into a checkable claim with evidence, gathers that evidence from several independent sources, and leaves a short list of real disagreements.

  1. A rubric. Plain one-line definitions, for example "archer: teaches Kyujutsu, and a Technique or the description centres on the bow". Checking a rating then means checking a fact.
  2. The app's own evidence, automatically. Skills, all five Techniques and the description of every School are already in the app, in our own words, checked against the books in earlier phases.
  3. The books, for what the app can't settle: a reading pass of each School's printed entry, with evidence in our own words and the page. This needs a session with your PDFs.
  4. A third opinion: the two community wikis the Sourcebook Index already links for every School, as a cross-check, never copied.
  5. Settle disagreements by evidence first. The few real judgement calls go to someone in your group as a short list, not 105 rows. Then a free real-world test: each player in your group takes the questionnaire as their character, and their School should be in their top six.
  6. Make it permanent: every rating keeps its evidence and confidence, and the tests check that each label meets the rubric.

Step 5: your group, for free

Ask each player to answer Try it as their own character. After the results, the page shows an answer code. Send me each code with that player's School (no names needed). A test then checks that every player's real School is in their top six.

Your part, as a non-expert: approve the rubric, spot-check about ten Schools by opening the cited page, and pass the short disagreement list to your group. If more than one of the ten is wrong, the audit is redone. Near-twins are handled inside the audit: wherever the evidence separates them, the difference becomes a label.

Hida Bushi

Crab Clan · Hida family (+1 Strength) · Bushi · School +1 Stamina · Honor 3.5 · Straightforward · Common · Core Rulebook p.106

Who they are

The Crab hold the great Wall on the Empire's southern border against the Shadowlands, and the Hida are their ruling family and the heart of their army. Their School is one of the oldest in Rokugan: wear heavy armour, take the blow, then answer with a heavy weapon such as the tetsubo. Other Clans, the Crane above all, think the Crab crude. The Crab think the other Clans have no idea what is out there.

How they play

The party's front line. Rank 1, The Way of the Crab: no Armor TN penalty for heavy armour (except for Stealth) and +1k0 damage with Heavy Weapons, so wear the heaviest armour you can. Rank 2 adds Reduction equal to your Earth Ring, which is why the build raises Earth.

Roleplaying them

  • You have seen what the rest of the Empire only fears. Patience with ceremony is short; patience with people who pull their weight is long.
  • Honor 3.5 is not dishonour. A Hida bends etiquette, not duty.
  • Intimidation is a social tool as well as a battle cry.
  • Good hooks: a sibling lost on the Wall, a grudge against the Crane, a debt to a Kuni shugenja.
  • Playing against type: a Hida who writes poetry about the Wall and is quietly good at it.

Questions to think about (optional): How long did you serve on the Wall, and what did you see there? Who do you trust most, and did they stand beside you there? What do you really think of the Crane? How do you expect to die, and does it frighten you?

Kuni Witch-Hunter

Crab Clan · Kuni family (+1 Intelligence) · Monk-type School · School +1 Willpower · Honor 5.5 · Some extra · Uncommon · The Great Clans p.40
Ask your GM. From The Great Clans p.40, so check your GM uses this book. This character shines only if the Taint and maho are part of the campaign, so ask about that too.

Who they are

The Kuni are the Crab's shugenja family, known for obsessive study of the Shadowlands. The Witch-Hunters are a small band of Kuni mystic warriors who roam the whole Empire, ignoring Clan borders, to hunt maho-tsukai and Shadowlands infiltrators. Commoners welcome them. Other samurai often do not.

How they play

An investigator who can fight. Rank 1, To See the Darkness: a Contested Roll (your Investigation/Awareness against their Sincerity/Willpower) senses the Taint in someone, and you gain +1k1 to resist the Taint and to attack Shadowlands creatures or known Tainted foes.

Roleplaying them

  • Most of the job is asking questions, watching faces and being unwelcome. The sword comes out last.
  • The great dilemma: a maho-tsukai may be a desperate, sympathetic person who made one terrible choice.
  • You answer to no local lord's comfort. That makes enemies, and occasionally saves a province.
  • Good hooks: the sorcerer who got away, a fellow hunter who fell to the Taint, a village that hid you.
  • Playing against type: a hunter who believes most of the Tainted can be saved.

Questions to think about (optional): Who was the first maho-tsukai you hunted, and what became of them? What would you do if someone you trust showed signs of the Taint? Which local lord would be glad to see you leave, and why?

How it would be built

  • Its own fragments, marker PART L PHASE 16 and one switch. No edits inside the wizard's fragments: the Clan-step button and "Suggested for…" hints wrap the wizard's render, as the Creation Cap of Four fix already does.
  • A declared dependency on the wizard (Phase 11.2) for Build. Without it the finder still works as a browser, with Build hidden.
  • No save-format change, and nothing written to the character except what Build applies through the wizard.
  • Tests: the reliability rules and floors; the app's rankings must match the prototype's (this page already matches it on 400 answer sets); Build equals doing it by hand; every guide's build passes in the wizard; phone layout; removal proof.

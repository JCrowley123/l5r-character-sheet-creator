Character Finder

An optional questionnaire for players who don't know what to play. About a dozen multiple-choice questions narrow the app's 105 Basic Schools down to one or two strong matches and a few more worth a look. Each match opens a guide, and a Build button hands the choice to the creation wizard.

What you decided

  • Every School is a candidate, not only the Great Clans and the Core Rulebook. A new player doesn't know what they don't know.
  • Two questions set the comfort level: how much rules detail, and how unusual a character. They change the order of results and never hide anything.
  • A guide, not a builder. The player decides what to build and makes every purchase.
  • GM warnings on niche or complex characters.
  • A Build button that feeds the creation wizard, so the player carries on with Skills and the rest.
  • A suggested starting build in every guide: Rings and Traits, Skills, Advantages and Disadvantages for a Rank 1 character.

How a player uses it

  1. Questions. Started from the wizard's Clan step ("Not sure? Help me choose") or the Characters screen. One question per screen, every one with "No preference", and "See my results now" from question 6.
  2. Results. One or two best matches, four more worth a look, and sometimes something different. "See all matches" lists all 105 in order.
  3. Guide. Who they are, how they play, how to roleplay them, a suggested starting build, where they can go later, and any reason to talk to the GM.
  4. Build. The wizard applies the Clan, Family and School cards itself, so Apply School still asks its own questions. The player lands on Rings & Traits, with a folded "Suggested for…" panel on the next steps.

Afterwards, optional "Bring them to life" prompts based on the book's Twenty Questions (pp. 100–102, in our own words) fill the existing Appearance, Personality, History and Notes boxes, so the save format does not change.

Two guides, written in full

These show the depth intended for every School. The facts come from the app's data. The roleplay advice is our own, and is the part to check hardest.

Hida Bushi

Crab Clan · Hida family (+1 Strength) · Bushi · School +1 Stamina · Honor 3.5 · Straightforward · Common · Core Rulebook p.106

Who they are

The Crab hold the great Wall on the Empire's southern border against the Shadowlands, and the Hida are their ruling family and the heart of their army. Their School is one of the oldest in Rokugan: wear heavy armour, take the blow, then answer with a heavy weapon such as the tetsubo. Other Clans, the Crane above all, think the Crab crude. The Crab think the other Clans have no idea what is out there.

How they play

The party's front line. Rank 1, The Way of the Crab: no Armor TN penalty for heavy armour (except for Stealth) and +1k0 damage with Heavy Weapons, so wear the heaviest armour you can. Rank 2 adds Reduction equal to your Earth Ring, which is why the build raises Earth.

Roleplaying them

  • You have seen what the rest of the Empire only fears. Patience with ceremony is short; patience with people who pull their weight is long.
  • Honor 3.5 is not dishonour. A Hida bends etiquette, not duty.
  • Intimidation is a social tool as well as a battle cry.
  • Good hooks: a sibling lost on the Wall, a grudge against the Crane, a debt to a Kuni shugenja.
  • Playing against type: a Hida who writes poetry about the Wall and is quietly good at it.

Kuni Witch-Hunter

Crab Clan · Kuni family (+1 Intelligence) · Monk-type School · School +1 Willpower · Honor 5.5 · Some extra · Uncommon · The Great Clans p.40
Ask your GM. From The Great Clans p.40, so check your GM uses this book. This character shines only if the Taint and maho are part of the campaign, so ask about that too.

Who they are

The Kuni are the Crab's shugenja family, known for obsessive study of the Shadowlands. The Witch-Hunters are a small band of Kuni mystic warriors who roam the whole Empire, ignoring Clan borders, to hunt maho-tsukai and Shadowlands infiltrators. Commoners welcome them. Other samurai often do not.

How they play

An investigator who can fight. Rank 1, To See the Darkness: a Contested Roll (your Investigation/Awareness against their Sincerity/Willpower) senses the Taint in someone, and you gain +1k1 to resist the Taint and to attack Shadowlands creatures or known Tainted foes.

Roleplaying them

  • Most of the job is asking questions, watching faces and being unwelcome. The sword comes out last.
  • The great dilemma: a maho-tsukai may be a desperate, sympathetic person who made one terrible choice.
  • You answer to no local lord's comfort. That makes enemies, and occasionally saves a province.
  • Good hooks: the sorcerer who got away, a fellow hunter who fell to the Taint, a village that hid you.
  • Playing against type: a hunter who believes most of the Tainted can be saved.

How it would be built

  • Its own fragments and marker (PART K PHASE 11.4), one switch, and no edits inside the wizard's fragments: the Clan-step button and "Suggested for…" panels wrap the wizard's render, as the Creation Cap of Four fix already does.
  • A declared dependency on the wizard (Phase 11.2) for Build. Without it the finder still works as a browser, with Build hidden.
  • No save-format change.
  • Tests: the adaptive order; the app's rankings match the prototype's on 500 fixed answer sets; every School reachable; GM notes match their rules; Build equals doing it by hand; every guide's build passes in the wizard; phone layout and touch targets; removal proof.

Your questions

Answer by number whenever suits you. Nothing is built until you approve.

  1. Name and place: Part K, Phase 11.4, "Character Finder"? Or another name, such as "Help me choose"?
  2. The one filter: should "No" to magic remove every Shugenja School, or only push them down the list?
  3. Entry points: the wizard's Clan step and the Characters screen. Anywhere else?
  4. Ratings: please check complexity and "how unusual" in All Schools. The checks re-run after any change.
  5. Question wording: edit freely. Should there also be a quick version with only the first six questions?
  6. Suggested builds: hints only, as drafted, or also a "Use this build" button? That would make it a builder rather than a guide.
  7. Roleplay prompts: into the existing Identity boxes (no save change), or a section of their own (a save-format change)?
  8. Ronin: fine to leave out until the planned ronin work adds ronin Schools?
  9. Guide order: batches as above, starting with Crab, Crane, Dragon and Lion?

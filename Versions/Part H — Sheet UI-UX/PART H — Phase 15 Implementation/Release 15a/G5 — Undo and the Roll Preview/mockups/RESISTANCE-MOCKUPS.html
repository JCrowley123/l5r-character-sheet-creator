Resisting something? Five mock-ups

Each design asks the one thing a player always knows: what the GM has just asked them to resist. The sheet then adds what the character has for it. All five are drawn inside the real app with the same character: Honour 6, Balance, Clear Thinker, and a Missing Limb choice that is not a resistance.

What all five share

  • Only effects you have something for. Every samurai sees Intimidation, Temptation and Fear, because Honour applies to them. Poison, Taint and the rest appear only when the character has a gift for them.
  • The sheet adds what certainly applies, once, and says so on a line: "Added: Honour +6 · Balance +1k0". The app's own list at the top of the preview still names each bonus with its page reference.
  • It asks when only the player can know. Clear Thinker is offered as a tick box, "only if they are trying to confuse or manipulate you". It is never added silently.
  • Choices that are not resistances stay as they are, like Missing Limb here.

Still yours to rule on: where each of the fifteen abilities sits, and whether it is added or asked. Clear Thinker under Temptation is an example, not a ruling. That table comes after you choose a design.

Rank 1 · recommended

A · Buttons in a row

The effects sit in one row of buttons. Tap one and it fills in; tap it again to clear it. The line below says what was added, and any "only if" ability is asked there.

For

  • Nothing to remember: the effects are named on every roll, with no tap to find them.
  • One tap to declare, one to clear.
  • Small. 218 px against 457 px today, and the Roll button stays on screen.
  • A gift shows up as its own button, so the button itself reminds you that you have it.
  • Large touch targets (44 px high).

Against

  • The row is there on every ordinary roll, including the many that resist nothing.
  • A character with several gifts gets two or three rows of buttons on a phone.
  • Which abilities sit behind a button shows only after the tap.
  • Buttons are a new control in the preview, where everything else is a tick box.
Rank 2

B · Open list that shows what each adds

Every effect is a row, always open, with the bonuses it would bring written under it. Choose one row.

For

  • The best reminder of the five: you see which abilities apply to what before you choose.
  • One tap to declare.
  • A plain list, like the rest of the preview.

Against

  • The tallest at rest: 310 px, and on this phone the Roll button starts 96 px off screen. That was the complaint A12 set out to fix.
  • One more row for every gift.
  • The same words repeat ("Honour +6" three times here).
  • A lot to carry on every roll that resists nothing.
Rank 3

C · One closed line

A12's line, asking about effects. Closed, it names the effects in small print; open, it lists them to choose from.

For

  • The smallest at rest (182 px); the Roll button stays on screen.
  • Reuses the line that is already built and passed your device checks.
  • Quiet on rolls that resist nothing.

Against

  • The choices are behind a tap, so two taps to declare.
  • Easy to pass over, which is the problem you want solved.
  • Open, it is the tallest of the five (425 px).
  • It goes against your earlier preference for play information in sight.
Rank 4

D · Drop-down

One box that reads "Nothing" until you pick an effect from the phone's own picker.

For

  • One line, however many effects the character has.
  • A standard control with keyboard and screen-reader support built in.

Against

  • At rest it says "Nothing", so it reminds you of nothing.
  • Two taps, and the phone's picker covers the preview while you choose.
  • The list cannot show what each effect adds.
  • Drop-downs are easy to overlook.
Rank 5

E · A Resist… button on the sheet

The question moves out of the preview. Tap Resist… on Rings & Traits, pick the effect, and the sheet opens the roll with the bonuses already in place.

For

  • Starts from the situation, which helps most when a player does not know which roll to make.
  • Ordinary rolls keep a clean preview.

Against

  • Needs a ruling on which roll each effect opens, and a GM may call for a different one.
  • A roll opened the usual way gets no help, so it needs one of the other designs as well.
  • A new control on the page; your earlier feedback was against permanent additions on the phone.
  • The largest build of the five.

Recommendation: A · Buttons in a row

  • It does what you asked for. A player with a poor memory sees the effects on every roll and taps the one the GM named. The sheet works out which abilities apply.
  • It is the cleanest that keeps everything in sight. C and D are as small but hide the choices; B shows more but pushes the Roll button off the phone's screen.
  • It answers the poison question. A gift adds a button; no gift, no button, and the roll is plain Earth.
  • B's strength is kept. The bonuses are named the moment you tap, and the app's own list at the top gives each one's page reference.
  • It closes no doors. E could be added later: Resist… would open the preview with a button already pressed.

What A costs: a row of buttons on every ordinary roll, and two or three rows for a character with several gifts. If that proves too much on your phone, C is the fallback with the same behaviour behind one line.
